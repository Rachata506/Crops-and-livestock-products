What can we learn?

Agricultural production varies across countries and products, reflecting different production levels, resources, and agricultural characteristics.

Understanding these patterns helps reveal how agricultural production is distributed worldwide, which countries lead production, and how land use and yield relate to agricultural output.

Using FAOSTAT data, the dashboard enables users to explore agricultural data, identify patterns, and gain insights into global agricultural production.

Key insight: growth came from yield, not land

Since 1961, crop output grew ×3.9 while harvested land grew only ×1.5.

Each hectare now produces about two and a half times as much as in 1961. Most of the extra food came from farming existing land better, not from farming much more of it.

Food security

Higher yields let crop output rise far faster than farmland, which is how supply kept growing.

Environment

Food grown on existing fields means less pressure to clear forests and natural habitats.

Policy

Where yields are still low, raising them can add output without needing new land.
