Why does this matter?

Food security, livelihoods and the economy all depend on agriculture, yet production differs widely from one country to another.

Climate, natural resources and farming practices make output very uneven around the world. Raw statistics hide these differences; charts and maps make them visible.

Food security

Knowing who produces what shows where food supply is secure and where it is at risk.

Livelihoods and economy

Farming supports millions of livelihoods and a large part of the global economy.

Uneven production

Climate, natural resources and farming practices differ, so countries produce very different amounts.

Data that is hard to read

FAOSTAT holds hundreds of thousands of records. Visualisation turns them into patterns anyone can read.

Key insight and potential impact

Output has grown about 3.9× since 1961, mostly because each hectare produces more, not because we farm far more land.

Between 1961 and 2024, harvested area grew far less than production. The difference is yield: tonnes produced per hectare.

All crops: output grew ×3.9 on 54% more harvested land, while yield per hectare grew ×2.5.

Smarter land use

Yield gains, not just new farmland, have helped feed a growing population. That matters for land use and the environment.

Finding the gaps

Regions with a small share of output (Africa produces about 11%) or low yields show where improvement may still be possible.

Better decisions

Policymakers, researchers and farmers can compare countries and spot gaps to support food-security planning.
